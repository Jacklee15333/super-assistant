## 📝 完整文字稿

Claude developer Anthropic has surpassed SpaceX's previous record IPO. While tech giants are pouring in billions, a key early investor is warning about the consequences of an unchecked AI race.

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share, beating the original target range of $70 to $78. Overwhelming demand pushed the public benefit corporation and its seven co-founders to sell roughly 980 million Class A common shares, up from a planned 825 million.

The offering raised $81.3 billion, according to a CNBC report, overtaking the previous record set by Elon Musk's SpaceX, which raised $75 billion in June. Anthropic's fully diluted market cap stands at about $1.5 trillion, while its market value excluding stock options sits closer to $1 trillion, according to the Wall Street Journal.

Shares begin trading Thursday on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol "ANT." The underwriting syndicate, including Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, also received a 30-day option to purchase an additional 147 million shares. Each of the seven co-founders is selling 3.38 million shares for a combined $1.96 billion, none of which flows to Anthropic itself.

### Claude Code sends revenue soaring as losses climb into the billions

Founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers led by CEO Dario Amodei, Anthropic saw explosive growth in its enterprise business in 2026, fueled by the coding tool Claude Code. Revenue surged about 1,200 percent year over year to $33.24 billion in the first nine months through September 30. By the end of October, annualized revenue had already reached roughly $81 billion.

Net losses for the same nine-month period widened from $25.1 billion a year earlier to $104 billion, driven primarily by revaluations of convertible notes and derivative liabilities. Amazon and Google rank among the largest shareholders, each holding stakes worth more than $100 billion. Together with Microsoft, Nvidia, and AMD, these tech giants are investing another $26 billion at the offering price. They also sell Anthropic expensive hardware, counting the company as a customer.

The IPO amounts to a stress test for the AI industry, which has recently been rattled by rising interest rates for data center developers, political headwinds from the U.S. midterm elections, and the ousting of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang last week. Anthropic itself clashed with the U.S. government twice this year over safety issues and lists risk factors across more than 90 pages in its prospectus. Rival OpenAI, meanwhile, has confidentially filed for an IPO planned for 2027.

### Early investor Dustin Moskovitz warns of an unchecked AI race

Amodei said in a roadshow video that the technology could usher in an age of wonders with careful risk management, but one of Anthropic's most important early investors disagrees. Facebook co-founder Dustin Moskovitz is sharply distancing himself from the IPO, as the New York Times reported.

Moskovitz invested between $15 million and $25 million in Anthropic's first funding round in 2021, after turning away from OpenAI. His goal was to help build a safety-focused research lab. On Bluesky, however, the former board observer called the IPO problematic and pointed to recent incidents in which AI agents coordinated cyberattacks on their own outside controlled environments. His funding of Anthropic "might well be an action that ended the world," Moskovitz wrote.

"Cari and I invested in Anthropic to promote safety, not racing," Moskovitz told the newspaper, referring to his wife Cari Tuna. To avoid personally profiting from the IPO, the couple transferred their multibillion-dollar stake to a nonprofit organization in early 2025. Dario and Daniela Amodei have not spoken with Moskovitz in more than a year, according to the Times' sources.
